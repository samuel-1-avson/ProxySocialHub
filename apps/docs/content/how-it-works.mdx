This page explains ProxyHubb without assuming you know anything about proxies, networking, or crypto. If you hit a word you don't know, check the [Glossary](/docs/glossary).

## 1. What is a proxy?

When you visit a website, it can see where your connection comes from: roughly which country and which internet company you use. That's your **IP address**.

A **proxy** is a middleman. Your traffic goes to the middleman, the middleman visits the website for you, and the website only sees the middleman.

<Flow caption="Without a proxy, the website sees you. With one, it sees the middleman.">
  <FlowStep icon="user" title="You">Want to visit a website</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="server" title="Middleman (proxy)" arrow="via">Makes the visit on your behalf</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="globe" title="Website" arrow="visits">Only sees the middleman's address</FlowStep>
</Flow>

## 2. Why a *home* connection?

<IdeaGrid>
  <IdeaCard icon="server" title="Data-centre proxy">
    The middleman is a big server farm. Cheap, but websites recognise these addresses easily and often block them or show different content.
  </IdeaCard>
  <IdeaCard icon="house" title="Residential proxy (ProxyHubb)">
    The middleman is an ordinary home internet connection. Websites treat it like a normal visitor, so you see what a real person in that place would see.
  </IdeaCard>
</IdeaGrid>

People and companies use this to check prices or ads in another country, test their own website from different places, or collect public information without being blocked.

## 3. The three players

<IdeaGrid>
  <IdeaCard icon="laptop" title="Buyers">
    Want to browse *as if* they were somewhere else. They pay for the data they send through a provider's home.
  </IdeaCard>
  <IdeaCard icon="house" title="Providers">
    Ordinary people with home internet. They install a small app that shares spare bandwidth, and they earn when buyers use it.
  </IdeaCard>
  <IdeaCard icon="server" title="ProxyHubb (the platform)">
    Sits in the middle: matches buyers with providers, passes the traffic through, counts usage, and makes sure everyone gets paid fairly.
  </IdeaCard>
</IdeaGrid>

Think of it like **Airbnb for spare home internet**: hosts list what they have, guests book it, and the platform handles the booking and the money.

## 4. One purchase, start to finish

Here is everything that happens when a buyer buys from the marketplace, in order.

<Steps>
<Step title="The provider gets ready">

The provider runs the ProxyHubb app on their computer. It "phones" ProxyHubb and keeps the line open, saying: *I'm online and I have spare bandwidth.*

<Flow>
  <FlowStep icon="house" title="Provider's computer">Runs the ProxyHubb app</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="server" title="ProxyHubb switchboard" arrow="keeps a line open">Now knows this home is available</FlowStep>
</Flow>

</Step>
<Step title="The buyer shops">

The buyer opens the ProxyHubb website, logs in with a crypto wallet (like Phantom, no password needed), and browses providers by country and price.

</Step>
<Step title="The buyer pays into a locked box">

The money doesn't go straight to the provider. It goes into a **locked box** on the Solana blockchain (called an *escrow*). The box has only two exits: to the provider, for data actually used, or back to the buyer. Nobody, including ProxyHubb, can send it anywhere else.

</Step>
<Step title="The buyer gets a key">

As soon as ProxyHubb sees the money is safely locked in, it gives the buyer a **username and password** for the proxy. That's their key.

</Step>
<Step title="The buyer connects">

The buyer turns on the ProxyHubb browser extension (one click), or pastes the username and password into their own code.

</Step>
<Step title="Traffic flows through the provider's home">

For every page the buyer visits:

<Flow caption="ProxyHubb checks the key on every visit, and counts how much data passes through.">
  <FlowStep icon="laptop" title="Buyer">Requests a page, with their key</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="server" title="Switchboard" arrow="request">Checks the key, picks the provider</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="house" title="Provider's home" arrow="forwards">Fetches the page</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="globe" title="Website" arrow="visits">Sends the page back the same way</FlowStep>
</Flow>

</Step>
<Step title="The meter runs">

The switchboard counts every bit of data that passes through, like a taxi meter.

</Step>
<Step title="Everyone gets paid fairly">

When the buyer is done (or their data runs out), ProxyHubb tells the locked box to settle up:

</Steps>

## 5. Three ways to buy

Not everyone buys from the marketplace. There are three ways to get a key:

<IdeaGrid>
  <IdeaCard icon="ticket" title="Quick pass" label="session intent">
    A temporary key you make in the dashboard in one click. Like a gym day pass. Uses the allowance from your Access Pass. Best for trying things out.
  </IdeaCard>
  <IdeaCard icon="calendar" title="Marketplace booking" label="order">
    Pick a specific provider, pay into the locked box, get a username and password. Like booking a specific Airbnb with a deposit.
  </IdeaCard>
  <IdeaCard icon="layers" title="Business fleet" label="bulk pool">
    Many keys managed under one company account, for software that needs lots of connections at once.
  </IdeaCard>
</IdeaGrid>

All three end up at the same door: the ProxyHubb switchboard. More detail: [Access paths](/docs/concepts/access-paths).

## 6. What's under the hood

If you're a developer, these are the real parts behind the story above:

<Flow caption="Three jobs: run the business, move the traffic, hold the money.">
  <FlowStep icon="office" title="The office">Accounts, listings, orders, keys. *Control API* (Go).</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="server" title="The switchboard" arrow="shares keys">Checks keys, routes traffic, counts data. *Edge gateway* (Rust).</FlowStep>
  <FlowStep icon="chain" title="The locked box" arrow="settles">Holds and releases payments. *Solana program*.</FlowStep>
</Flow>

| Plain name | Real name | Where you'll meet it |
|------------|-----------|----------------------|
| The website | Web app | `proxyhubb.com` |
| The office | Control API | `api.proxyhubb.com` |
| The switchboard | Edge gateway | `proxy.proxyhubb.com`: door **8090** for buyers, door **3128** for providers |
| The provider app | `provider-node` + `proxyhubb-cli` | Installed on the provider's computer |
| The locked box | Solana escrow program | Solana devnet (test network) |
| The browser add-on | Chrome / Firefox extension | Chrome Web Store |

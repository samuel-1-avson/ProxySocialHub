# Why Providers Need a Cleaner Path

## The invisible other half
Most conversations about residential proxies start with the buyer.

Buyers need routes through real household networks. They care about access, quality, and whether billing matches what they used.

But every buyer route has a supply side. Someone has to provide that capacity.

## The provider frustration
People with unused or underused residential bandwidth rarely have a clean way to participate.

Typical options feel like:
1. Become a full infrastructure operator yourself
2. Join another opaque middleman
3. Sell capacity through private chats and trust handshakes
4. Accept unclear rules about usage, payouts, and discovery

That is not a real marketplace. It is improvisation.

## What “providing a proxy” should feel like
Providing capacity should feel closer to quiet infrastructure:

1. Connect with a wallet
2. Run a node
3. Publish a listing
4. Stay online and healthy
5. Carry real traffic when buyers route through your capacity
6. See usage and earnings in a way you can reason about

You should not need to invent your own billing portal, discovery channel, and trust system from scratch.

## Why ProxyHubb was built with providers as participants
ProxyHubb was built so providers are not invisible supply behind a reseller brand.

The thesis:
- discovery instead of secrecy
- metered usage instead of opaque invoices
- wallet-native settlement instead of locked billing portals alone
- providers as marketplace participants who publish capacity by running a node

## Emotional north star for this video
The goal is not “passive income overnight.”
The goal is not crypto hype.

The goal is to show how residential bandwidth can enter a market with clearer rules — and how a provider node is the practical machine that makes that possible.

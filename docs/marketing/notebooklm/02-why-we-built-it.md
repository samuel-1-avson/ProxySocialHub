# Why ProxyHubb Was Built

## The invisible layer
Most people never think about residential bandwidth as a market. But many modern workflows depend on it: research, testing, automation, and other work that needs to reach the web through real household networks rather than datacenter IPs.

That layer is infrastructure. Important. Mostly invisible. Often poorly packaged.

## The frustration that started the project
Buying residential proxies often feels like this:

1. Find a seller
2. Send money
3. Hope the routes work
4. Struggle to compare quality
5. Struggle to audit usage
6. Have little recourse when something fails

On the other side, people with unused or underused bandwidth rarely have a clean way to publish capacity into a real market. Monetizing that capacity usually means becoming an infrastructure operator yourself, or joining another opaque middleman.

## The founding thesis
Bandwidth should not feel like a handshake in the dark.

It should feel like quiet infrastructure with clearer rules:
- transparent enough for a non-technical buyer to understand
- serious enough for a technical buyer to trust
- open enough for providers to participate by running a node

## Why not just another proxy reseller?
A reseller still keeps the black box.

ProxyHubb was built to change the structure of the market itself:
- discovery instead of secrecy
- metered usage instead of opaque invoices
- wallet-native settlement instead of locked billing portals
- providers as participants, not invisible supply

## The emotional north star
The goal is not hype.
The goal is not “crypto for its own sake.”

The goal is to make residential bandwidth feel like a real marketplace — auditable, two-sided, and understandable — while still being useful infrastructure for people who just need routes that work.

## One line for the video
I built ProxyHubb because residential bandwidth deserves a market with clearer rules, not another black box.

# The Build Adventure

Use this document as the narrative backbone for a founder / builder story video.

## Act 1 — The problem becomes personal
The project did not begin as “let’s launch a crypto brand.”

It began with a practical frustration: residential bandwidth is important internet plumbing, but the market around it feels closed. Buyers struggle to trust what they are buying. Providers struggle to participate cleanly. Usage and billing are hard to inspect.

That gap became the reason to build.

## Act 2 — The idea sounds simple
Build a marketplace where:
- buyers can discover residential capacity
- providers can publish it by running a node
- traffic is measured at the edge
- settlement is wallet-native on Solana
- the network is inspectable in public

Simple to say. Hard to build.

## Act 3 — Building the full stack
The adventure was not one feature. It was many layers arriving together.

### Marketplace
A place to browse capacity, create orders, and manage the buy/sell relationship like a real market instead of a private reseller list.

### Control plane
Auth, wallets, listings, orders, provider operations — the system that makes the market callable and governable through an API and web app.

### Edge gateway
The part that makes the product real: buyer sessions come in, traffic is admitted, and usage can be measured where it actually happens.

### Provider node
A daemon providers can run to advertise capacity and carry traffic. Designed so people can join from ordinary networks, including situations where inbound ports are not practical.

### Developer surfaces
SDKs, CLI, docs — because infrastructure products only become real when builders can integrate them.

### Public explorer
Transparency as a product surface: inspect nodes, sessions, and network activity without needing an account.

## Act 4 — What the weeks felt like
Some weeks felt like product design: how do buyers understand capacity, pricing, and next steps?

Some weeks felt like systems engineering: how do sessions route, how is usage measured, how do providers stay connected?

Most weeks were both.

That dual nature is the story of ProxyHubb: a product people can understand, backed by infrastructure that has to work under real traffic.

## Act 5 — Where it stands
ProxyHubb is live and early.

The core idea is shipped in usable form:
- marketplace
- wallet-native access
- buyer routing through the edge
- provider nodes
- docs and SDKs
- public explorer

It is still shipping. Still learning. Still turning an opaque bandwidth market into quieter, clearer infrastructure.

## Closing line options for narration
- Bandwidth should feel less like a handshake in the dark, and more like a market with clear rules.
- Quiet infrastructure for residential proxy bandwidth.
- Built for buyers who need routes that work — and providers who want a real way to participate.
